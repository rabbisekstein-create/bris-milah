Questions men ask

Is my brit complete?

Many men notice at some point that they look different from other Jewish men and wonder whether their brit was done fully. It is a common question, and it usually has a simple answer.

A very common question

Men often notice it while growing up, seeing that brothers or friends look different. Others learn later that a hospital circumcision is not the same as a brit, or were once told that something was not done fully. Naturally, they wonder: is this right? And why does mine look different?

There are a few normal reasons this happens, explained below. Searching online tends to give conflicting answers, mostly from people who are not mohalim. A short, private conversation with a mohel usually settles it.

General information onlyEvery case is different and is decided in person, according to halacha. This page gives general information only.

When a brit may not be complete

First, a reassurance. If your brit was done by a qualified mohel and checked afterwards to make sure it healed well, it is done. There is no need to keep examining it or worrying about it for the rest of your life. A real question only arises when it was not done properly, or when nobody ever checked afterwards that it healed as it should.

A simple way to picture it. Think of a hand in a sleeve: the hand stands for the head, and the sleeve for the skin over it.

A picture like this is only a rough guide. How it looks at rest is not the whole picture: build, size, and how the brit was done in the first place all make a difference. If it was done by an experienced mohel, what looks like extra skin is very often perfectly fine. The only way to know is to ask a mohel, who can tell you in person.

Too much skin was left. If the head still looks largely covered, it is worth having it checked. Occasionally a brit was not done fully, even at a proper bris; more often, it turns out to be fine.

The skin healed back. Sometimes the brit was done properly, but during healing the skin re-attached or grew forward again.

It was done in a hospital. A circumcision done by a doctor as a medical procedure is generally not a brit milah. In most cases a hatafas dam bris completes it, and if too much skin was left, that is corrected at the same time.

And sometimes it is fine. Quite often, what looks different turns out to be perfectly complete, and men are relieved to hear it from someone who has nothing to gain by saying so.

How it is checked

It starts with a private conversation, by phone or on WhatsApp. You tell me what you have noticed and what you know about how your brit was done, and I tell you honestly whether it sounds like something that needs to be seen.

If it does, we meet in person. A short, private examination is enough to see exactly what is there. I explain what I see in plain words, and what, if anything, needs to be done.

If a correction is needed, it can usually be done in the same visit, so you only need to come once. If you would rather go home and think about it first, that is completely fine.

What a correction involves

It is done under local anesthesia, privately and without rushing. The remaining skin is removed so that the brit is complete, and it is done carefully, so the result looks neat and natural. Nothing healthy is removed that does not need to be.

Most men are comfortable again within ten days to two weeks, and healing is complete after about four weeks. Aftercare is explained in advance, and you have my own cell number for any question along the way.

Cost should never be the reason to stay unsure. Nobody is turned away over money; when needed, a sponsor covers it.

Short answers

My brit was done by a mohel. Can it still be incomplete?

Yes, it happens. Sometimes too much skin was left, or the skin healed in a way that covers more than it should. The only way to know is for a mohel to see it in person.

I was circumcised in a hospital, not at a bris. Do I need anything?

Usually yes. A circumcision done by a doctor as a medical procedure is generally not a brit milah. In most cases a hatafas dam bris completes it, and if too much skin was left, that is corrected at the same time.

How do I know whether it is worth the trip?

Start with a private phone call or WhatsApp message. Tell me what you have noticed, and I will tell you honestly whether it sounds like something that needs to be seen.

Does a correction hurt?

It is done under local anesthesia, so the area is numb during the procedure. Pain management for the following days is planned in advance.

How long is the recovery?

Most men are comfortable again within ten days to two weeks, and healing is complete after about four weeks. Recovery is planned around your work and your life.

Will you remove the frenulum?

No. The frenulum is not part of the brit, and it is not something that is removed. Often, what people call the frenulum is really extra skin left on the underside. That extra skin is removed as part of completing the brit, which is what gives a neat, finished result. If the frenulum itself is genuinely too tight, it can be loosened during the same procedure.

Will anyone have to know?

No. Our conversations are confidential, and if you prefer to keep it private, nobody else needs to know.
