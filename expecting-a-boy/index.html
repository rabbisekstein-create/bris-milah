Expecting a Boy?

Planning your son’s bris — and the two things to do now.

Mazel tov! A bris usually takes place on the eighth day after a baby boy is born. That first week goes by quickly, so it helps to have your mohel arranged before the birth. You don’t need to plan anything else yet.

Save my contact Tell me your due date (WhatsApp)

What to do now

  1. Save my number

    Tap “Save my contact” and my name, phone and WhatsApp go straight into your phone. When the baby comes, you won’t have to search for anyone.

  2. Tell me your due date

    Send me a short WhatsApp message with your due date and where you live. I’ll note it, and you can ask me anything in the meantime.

When your son is born

Send me a message

Send me a message as soon as you’re able to — day or night is fine. Let me know the date and the time he was born. The time matters for counting the eighth day, especially if he’s born in the evening.

Before the bris

We plan it together

We’ll speak about the date, the time and the place, and I’ll explain what to have ready. You can also see my checklists.

If your baby’s doctor says to wait, we wait. There’s no pressure. We simply set a new date when he’s ready.

Questions families ask

Do we need to belong to a shul?

No. I’m happy to help any Jewish family, whatever your background.

This is the first bris in our family. Where do we start?

That’s very common. I’ll guide you step by step, from what to prepare to what happens during the ceremony.

Where can the bris take place?

At home, in a shul, in a hall, or another place that works for your family.
